Current Directions

My recent work models best bid and ask dynamics with Hawkes processes. With Kiseop Lee, I developed a spread-gated Hawkes-flocking model of the four best-quote moves (bid and ask, up and down), in which the current spread switches cross-side “flocking” excitation on and off. In limit order book data, the flocking effect shows up as a millisecond snap-back of the spread after it widens, and the model leads to a rule for placing a limit order: at the best or second-best price, and in what quantity. A companion paper studies how the definition of a price-change event affects Hawkes estimates of clustering in the consolidated US quote feed.

Building on these, I am working on when to execute an order given real-time forecasts of price direction, and on detecting changes in directional price dynamics as they happen. A second line of work, partly with students, develops online monitoring of covariance structure in high-dimensional financial return panels, with calibrated false-alarm control. I also work with students on forecasting energy prices with statistical and deep learning models.
